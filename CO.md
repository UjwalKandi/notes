You arrive in Denver at 9:35 PM MDT and have about two hours of driving and errands before sleeping at the RMNP entrance. No photography tonight. The sole objective is to be parked at Beaver Meadows by midnight, rested enough to drive into the park by 5:00 AM.

The Beaver Meadows Visitor Center lot is a large, well-lit, paved lot that photographers use routinely for pre-dawn park access. You will not be the only car sleeping there.

## TUESDAY, JUNE 16 — TRAIL RIDGE ROAD + BRECKENRIDGE + TWIN LAKES + INDEPENDENCE PASS + ASPEN + MAROON BELLS SCOUT

### Day Overview
Tuesday is the longest day in terms of elevation change — you start in Rocky Mountain National Park at 12,183 feet, descend to Aspen at 7,908 feet, climb back over Independence Pass at 12,095 feet, and end up sleeping at 9,000 feet on Maroon Creek Road. It is the alpine day and the Trail Ridge Road section is some of the most dramatic high-altitude driving in North America.

The sunrise session at Many Parks Curve at 5:31 AM captures the layered alpine ridges that RMNP is defined by. You are already parked 5 minutes from this viewpoint. After Trail Ridge Road, you traverse the park's western side, exit near Grand Lake, and head south through the Summit County corridor — passing through Breckenridge for a 30-minute walk through the historic Victorian downtown. Breckenridge at 10:50 AM is not golden hour photography, but the town itself, the Tenmile Range behind it, and the Blue River corridor are worth 30 minutes as you pass through.

Twin Lakes offers midday mountain reflection. Independence Pass at 12,095 feet is one of the highest paved roads in America. Aspen is a rest and food stop. The evening session at Maroon Bells (5:25–8:30 PM) is tonight's golden hour — and critically, you scout your exact sunrise position for Wednesday's 5:33 AM shoot. Then you drive 3 miles back down Maroon Creek Road and sleep in a Forest Service pullout. Tomorrow morning you drive 7 minutes and walk to your pre-scouted position. No last-minute rushing.

### Tuesday Photography Detail

**5:31 AM — Many Parks Curve (9,620 ft)**
Park entry: $35/vehicle or Annual Pass. Gate opens 5:00 AM.

Multiple glacially carved mountain parks (wide alpine meadows) spread across the valley below at different elevations. Each successive ridgeline is a slightly different luminance value — warm orange foreground to cool blue middle distance to pale grey far ridges. This layered tonal depth is what makes the composition work.

- **70–200mm** — compress 4–5 ridgelines into stacked layers. This is the shot.
- **16–24mm** — full panorama with foreground tundra and the road curving into the frame as a leading line
- Temperature at 9,600 ft in June is 35–45°F. Packable down jacket essential.
- Wind is minimal before 6 AM. Light shifts fast — shoot through the full transition.

**6:30 AM — Forest Canyon Overlook**
A 2,500-foot U-shaped glacially carved canyon directly below the overlook. The canyon floor river catches first light before the walls are illuminated — a brief window of lit water against dark canyon. Telephoto for the canyon floor. Wide for the full canyon width with tundra as foreground.

**7:15 AM — Alpine Visitor Center tundra**
At 11,796 ft, this is above treeline. The tundra in early June has alpine wildflowers just beginning — tiny yellow and white blooms, some against remnant snow patches. A low-angle macro composition of alpine blooms against white snow is a frame unique to this elevation and this season. The boardwalk keeps you on-trail. Spend time here — you are standing on a landscape most people never reach.

**8:15 AM — Milner Pass**
The Continental Divide. Simple graphic: the sign, the saddle, two watersheds draining opposite directions. Shoot the divide marker against blue sky — then walk 100 yards to see the infant Colorado River on the west side. That same water eventually reaches the Gulf of California.

**10:50 AM — Breckenridge**
Exit US-40 onto CO-9 south. Breckenridge is 8 miles off your direct route — total detour adds 30 minutes including the stop.

This is a mid-morning town stop, not a golden hour shoot. The photography is architectural and documentary: the Victorian commercial buildings on Main Street date to the 1880s gold rush. The Tenmile Range (including Quandary Peak at 14,265 ft) rises behind the south end of town. The Blue River runs along the west edge of the historic district.

- Main Street wide angle — buildings receding, mountains framing the end of the street
- Victorian storefront detail — ornamental brickwork, painted wood facades
- Blue River from the main bridge — a quick composition before moving on
- This is 30 minutes of exploration and documentation, not portfolio-grade golden hour work. Treat it accordingly.

**12:15 PM — Twin Lakes**
Two glacially carved lakes at the base of the Collegiate Peaks. Mount Elbert (14,440 ft, Colorado's highest) and Mount Massive (14,421 ft) reflect in still water. Midday light is flat, but the reflection quality is about wind, not light angle. If conditions are calm, the reflection is strong. One hour maximum.

**1:15 PM — Independence Pass (12,095 ft)**
One of the highest paved roads in North America. At the summit: panoramic views of the Sawatch Range with the road switchbacking down both sides visible. The primary subject is the road itself — the asphalt ribbon cutting through bare alpine terrain. Wide angle for the road curving away toward Aspen. The summit also has a short walk to a scenic viewpoint above the parking area.

**5:25–8:30 PM — Maroon Bells Evening**
The bell-shaped peaks face northwest — they catch warm afternoon and evening light. This session is dual purpose:

*Photography:* The peaks at golden hour (7:45–8:20 PM) go from warm orange to deep amber. The aspen trees along the lake shore reflect the warm light. This is a strong golden hour session in its own right.

*Scouting:* Walk the full lakeshore. Find the position on the northeast bank where both peaks reflect simultaneously. Test angles. Mark your tripod spot. Note what other photographers are doing and find a position that differs. Tomorrow morning you will be at this exact spot in the dark at 5:00 AM.

The day moves from the most iconic Colorado mountain shot to the darkest, deepest canyon in the state.

The Maroon Bells session works because of Tuesday night's preparation: you scouted the position, you know where to stand, you slept 7 minutes away, and you walk in before anyone else. This is the exact model for getting genuinely strong landscape photographs on a fast trip — the preparation is what separates a good shot from a lucky one.

After Maroon Bells, you drive southeast through Aspen valley (morning light on CO-82 is genuinely cinematic), stop briefly at Blue Mesa Reservoir (already on your route, no detour), and arrive at Black Canyon mid-morning. Black Canyon is a full afternoon and evening location — the canyon's extreme depth and dark Precambrian rock make it a different visual experience from any other stop on the trip. Chasm View at sunset is the last golden hour session of Wednesday.

### Wednesday Photography Detail

**5:33 AM — Maroon Bells Lake Reflection**
*Self-drive before 8 AM — no reservation needed before gate activates.*

The Maroon Bells are two 14,000-ft peaks — the Maroon Peak (14,156 ft) and North Maroon Peak (14,014 ft) — with June snow on the upper slopes, green aspen shoreline, and still water below. The reflection window is strongest 4:45–6:30 AM before wind develops. At 5:33 AM, first light hits the upper spires while the lake surface remains in cool blue shadow — this warm/cool contrast is the defining frame.

- **16–24mm** — full reflection, both peaks, aspen framing both sides of the lake. The standard composition. Execute it well.
- **50mm** — cleaner sky-to-reflection ratio, less distortion
- **70–200mm** — isolate peak with snow couloir detail
- Shoot RAW. The dynamic range from deep shadow to illuminated peak summit exceeds JPEG latitude.
- The still water window closes as soon as wind begins. If you see the reflection breaking up, shoot fast and accept the rippled version — it has its own character.

**CO-82 Aspen Valley Road (8:00–8:45 AM)**
Heading east out of Aspen, CO-82 runs through a spectacular mountain corridor. Castle Peak and the Elk Mountains rise to the south. Morning sidelight is still strong at 8 AM. Drive slowly, shoot from pullouts. This is 45 minutes of road photography unique to this valley — it's not a destination stop, it's the connective tissue between Maroon Bells and Black Canyon, and it photographs well.

**Curecanti NRA / Blue Mesa Reservoir (~10:00 AM)**
Colorado's largest reservoir sits directly on US-50 — zero detour. Pull at the first marked overlook. Deep blue water against pale canyon walls in morning light. The color contrast is strong. 15 minutes, then continue.

**11:30 AM — Gunnison Point, Black Canyon**
Entry: $35/vehicle or Annual Pass. The canyon drops 2,722 feet to the Gunnison River. The rock is Precambrian gneiss and schist — 1.8 billion years old, some of the oldest exposed rock on Earth — and it is almost black against the pale sky. The visual weight of this canyon is completely different from the Grand Canyon or Black Canyon of the Yellowstone. It is darker, narrower, and more oppressive.

- Wide angle for the full canyon drop from rim to river
- Telephoto to compress the canyon layers — the alternating dark rock and lighter intrusion bands visible across the void
- At midday the canyon floor is in full shadow while the rim is in flat light. This still works because of the extreme luminance contrast.

**1:00 PM — Painted Wall**
Colorado's tallest cliff face at 2,250 ft. White and pink pegmatite veins streak through the dark gneiss face on the opposite canyon wall — they resemble paint brushstrokes from the overlook, hence the name. Use telephoto (200mm+) to bring the vein detail across the canyon void. The scale is difficult to communicate in a photograph; include the canyon floor far below as a depth reference.

**6:00–8:30 PM — Chasm View**
The best canyon sunset position on the South Rim. The canyon runs roughly east-west — at sunset, light rakes across the southern canyon wall face from the west. The canyon shadows deepen from dark brown to near-black while the rim rocks above hold warm orange light. At peak golden hour (7:45–8:20 PM), the lit rim against the black canyon void is the frame. After sunset (8:20–8:30 PM), the canyon fills with deep blue while the sky above holds a warm gradient — stay for these final 10 minutes. They are often the strongest.

This backtrack of 13 miles each way is the only intentional backtrack on the full trip and it is entirely justified — Red Mountain Pass at sunset is one of the strongest golden hour frames in Colorado.

### Thursday Photography Detail

**5:35 AM — Ouray Mountain Bowl Sunrise**
Ouray sits at 7,792 ft in a natural mountain amphitheater enclosed on three sides by peaks reaching 13,000–14,000 ft. The Amphitheater Campground area south of town, or the overlook on the Perimeter Trail above town, gives the full bowl view. At sunrise, the upper peaks catch warm alpenglow while the town below remains in cool shadow — the peaks are lit, the valley is dark, and the town sits between the two worlds. Short walk from the depot or any central Ouray parking.

**7:15 AM — Blue Lakes Hike**
*Commitment level: 3.5–4 hours. The most physically demanding stop on the trip.*

Dallas Creek Road (dirt, 4 miles from US-550, passable in any standard vehicle) leads to the trailhead. The trail gains 1,800 feet over 2.75 miles to the lower lake, with additional gain to the upper lakes at approximately 12,000 feet. In mid-June, the upper lakes have snow and ice on their margins with turquoise glacially-fed water below. The Sneffels Wilderness and Mount Sneffels (14,158 ft) form the backdrop.

The photography here is unlike any other location on the trip: vivid turquoise water, remnant snowpack, gray-black granite walls, and a 14,000-ft peak reflected in the lake surface. The color saturation is extreme — the blue-green of the water against white snow and dark rock is the most saturated frame on the entire five-day itinerary.

- Wide angle from the shoreline — foreground snow and rock at water's edge, mountain behind
- Telephoto — compress Mount Sneffels against the lake surface
- **Altitude advisory:** You are at 12,000 ft after flying from Austin (620 ft) four days ago. Hydrate aggressively before and during the hike. If headache or nausea develops, descend. Do not push through altitude sickness symptoms.

**Bear Creek Falls — 11:33 AM**
US-550, 3 miles south of Ouray. Roadside pullout, 10-minute stop. The falls pour off the canyon wall directly beside the highway. At peak June snowmelt, the volume is impressive. Shoot the falls with the canyon walls and highway guard rail providing context.

**11:50 AM — Red Mountain Pass (first pass — midday)**
Scout your golden hour positions. Walk the pullouts on both sides of the summit. The southward view toward Silverton with the highway curving away and the three Red Mountains flanking the road is the primary composition. Identify the exact pullout with the clearest southwest sightline for the 7:45 PM session.

The iron oxide in the surrounding rock gives the Red Mountains their vivid rust-orange and deep red coloring. This is not typical Colorado brown-grey granite — it is genuinely red, and in golden light it reads as unnaturally saturated. Midday light is flat but the color still reads strongly. Take establishing shots now, return for the golden hour sequence.

**12:40 PM — Silverton**
A preserved 1880s silver mining town at 9,318 ft enclosed on all sides by 13,000-ft peaks. The entire downtown is on the National Register of Historic Places.

*Blair Street* — The historic commercial district. Intact Victorian storefronts from the 1880s. Afternoon sidelight (1–3 PM) creates strong shadow definition on the ornate facades.

*Greene Street* — The main street with operating saloons, a historic hotel, and original commercial buildings still in use.

*The Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad depot* — The terminus of the historic steam railroad from Durango. If a train is in the station, the steam locomotive against the mountain backdrop is a strong frame.

*Mountain backdrop from town* — Every direction from Silverton's main street shows 13,000-ft peaks. The visual enclosure of the town is what makes it distinctive — you are deep inside the mountains, not approaching them.

**4:55 PM — Red Mountain Pass (golden hour session)**
You return north from Silverton specifically for this. The 13-mile drive takes 25 minutes, positioning you at the pass by 4:55 PM for a 3.5-hour golden hour session.

The three Red Mountains (Red Mountain #1, #2, #3) shift through a color sequence from 7:45–8:30 PM that most photographers have never seen on a mountain:
- **7:45–8:00 PM:** Warm orange — strong directional sidelight across the oxidized rock face
- **8:00–8:15 PM:** Deep crimson-red — the saturation peak. The mountains are the color of rust at scale.
- **8:15–8:25 PM:** Purple-red transition — final direct light, the last warm frames
- **8:25–8:30 PM:** Shadow falls — the last indirect light, sky still holds gradient

The highway curving south toward Silverton with the red peaks in the background is the defining composition. Also look for: the road crossing the iron-stained stream below the pass (the water is a vivid orange-red from iron oxide runoff — a genuinely unusual natural phenomenon).

After 8:30 PM: **continue south on US-550 toward Durango. Do not turn north.**

## FRIDAY, JUNE 19 — DURANGO DAWN + MESA VERDE + ROYAL GORGE + COLORADO SPRINGS + FAN FEST → GREAT SAND DUNES

### Day Overview
Friday is the longest driving day of the trip and the most culturally and visually diverse. It begins at blue hour in Durango's Victorian railroad town, moves to Mesa Verde National Park's 700-year-old cliff dwellings, detours to Royal Gorge Bridge (1,000-ft gorge), arrives in Colorado Springs for Garden of the Gods, then anchors the evening at the Pikes Peak Fan Fest during golden hour before driving south to sleep at Great Sand Dunes.

The day holds the Fan Fest — the reason for the specific June 19 date — and it delivers the most distinct photography variety of any single day: steam locomotives, ancient cliff architecture, a suspension bridge over a canyon, red sandstone formations, race cars against a 14,000-ft mountain. No other day on the trip covers this range of subjects.

Royal Gorge is an addition to the original plan. The Arkansas River drops 1,000 feet below the Royal Gorge Bridge, and the bridge crosses directly over that drop. It is slightly off the direct Mesa Verde to Colorado Springs route but falls naturally into the Cañon City area as you drive north toward Colorado Springs. The addition compresses Garden of the Gods from 1 hour to 30 minutes (Siamese Twins only), but Royal Gorge is worth that trade.

The Fan Fest golden hour (7:45–8:21 PM) is the most deliberate golden hour setup of the entire trip — it happens in a controlled outdoor environment with specific subjects (race cars), a known backdrop (Pikes Peak), and a predicted light window. Plan every shot before you arrive.

### Friday Photography Detail

**5:15–6:00 AM — Durango Blue Hour + D&SNG Steam Locomotive**
You slept in the depot lot. Walk out of the car into your photography location.

Durango's Main Avenue is lined with intact 1880s Victorian commercial buildings — ornate brick facades, cast-iron details, original storefronts — directly across from the historic Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad depot. At blue hour (5:15–5:45 AM), the street lamps are still lit against a deep blue pre-dawn sky. The combination of warm amber building light and cool blue exterior creates a natural color contrast that disappears once the sun rises. This window is approximately 30 minutes.

- **Wide angle down Main Avenue** — Victorian buildings receding into the distance, mountains framing the end of the street, street lamps still burning
- **D&SNG Depot building** — The 1881 Victorian depot at 479 Main Ave. The locomotive is brought out and prepped before the first 8:15 AM departure. By 5:45 AM, engine house activity may already be visible through the yard fence.
- **Steam locomotive detail** — Telephoto on the boiler, wheel assembly, and cab against the pre-dawn sky. Even before the train moves, the iron structure reads powerfully in low light.
- **Storefront window reflections** — The blue morning sky reflected in plate glass storefronts with warm interior light behind.

**6:45–11:00 AM — Mesa Verde National Park**
Entry: $35/vehicle or America the Beautiful Annual Pass. Cliff Palace ranger tour: $5/person — booked on Recreation.gov in advance.

Every other location on this trip is geological — canyons, mountains, sandstone, dunes. Mesa Verde introduces human architecture from 700–900 years ago built directly into the canyon walls. This visual shift is the point. Cliff Palace is a 150-room dwelling constructed between 1190–1270 CE, set in a south-facing natural alcove that receives direct morning light from the east. In June, the alcove face is well illuminated by 8:00 AM.

*Sun Point Overlook (7:20–8:00 AM)*
Faces south and east across the Montezuma Valley with the San Juan Mountains visible on the horizon. The canyon system below catches warm morning light. The cultural elements are small in the canyon, dwarfed by the geological scale — this contrast tells the Mesa Verde story in a single frame. Wide angle for the full panorama.

*Cliff Palace (8:00–9:30 AM)*
From the overlook: Telephoto (200mm+) compresses the dwelling across the canyon void. The white and tan plaster walls, kiva openings, and tower structures are illuminated in warm directional light at 8 AM. The overlook requires no reservation and gives the cleanest, least-crowded composition.

On the ranger tour (if booked): You descend into the canyon and stand at the base of the 150-room complex. The four-story tower fills the frame from below. Wide angle looking up captures the full dwelling height against the sandstone alcove ceiling above — this composition is only available on the tour and it communicates the engineering feat in a way the overlook cannot.

*Spruce Canyon Overlook + Chapin Mesa loop (9:30–10:30 AM)*
Multiple additional dwelling sites are visible from the mesa-top road. Drive the 6-mile archaeological loop, stop at pullouts. The canyon views with dwelling structures embedded in the walls continue throughout the loop.

**2:30–3:30 PM — Royal Gorge Bridge**
The Arkansas River drops 1,000 feet below the Royal Gorge Bridge — the gorge is 50 feet wide at the river with walls rising nearly vertically on both sides. The bridge crosses directly above this drop.

The Royal Gorge Bridge & Park charges admission ($30/person). The bridge itself is a 1929 suspension bridge at 956 feet above the river. Walk to the center of the bridge and look both directions — upstream toward the granite walls narrowing, downstream toward the river emerging from the gorge. The engineering scale and the geological scale compete in every frame.

- **Looking straight down from center bridge** — The Arkansas River as a thread 956 feet below. Wide angle, include both sets of cables for depth.
- **Along the bridge span** — Telephoto compression of the cable structure receding into the gorge wall
- **Canyon walls from the rim** — The vertical granite walls 50 feet apart at river level, shot from the rim walkway above. Include the river as a thin stripe far below.
- Midday light is flat at 2:30 PM, but the gorge depth communicates powerfully in any light — the darkness of the canyon floor against lit walls is a natural contrast.

**4:45–5:00 PM — Garden of the Gods — Siamese Twins viewpoint only**
Time is compressed to 30 minutes. Go directly to the Siamese Twins.

*Siamese Twins* — Two rock formations create a natural arch that perfectly frames Pikes Peak (14,115 ft) in the gap between them. This is the essential Garden of the Gods photograph and the reason to stop. Use a telephoto (200mm+) from the marked viewpoint to bring the mountain through the arch. Afternoon light from the west at 4:45 PM is workable — the red sandstone is side-lit. 20 minutes at this single viewpoint is enough for the shot.

**5:00–9:00 PM — Pikes Peak Fan Fest, Downtown Colorado Springs**
The Pikes Peak International Hill Climb is one of the oldest motorsport events in the world, running since 1916. Fan Fest brings the race cars into downtown Colorado Springs — unlimited hillclimbers, electric prototypes, production cars, WRC-spec vehicles on public display. Pikes Peak (14,115 ft) is visible directly to the west from downtown.

| 5:00–7:00 PM | Afternoon, flat | Car detail, team paddock, crowd energy, mechanics working |
| 7:00–7:45 PM | Pre-golden, warm directional | Car portraits with mountain, engineering close-ups |
| **7:45–8:21 PM** | **★★ Golden hour** | **Race cars in warm light + Pikes Peak backdrop — the defining frame** |

Camera setup for Fan Fest:
- **70–200mm f/2.8** — Car detail compression, crowd portraits, Pikes Peak background separation. Primary lens for the golden hour window.
- **16–24mm** — Environmental storytelling, paddock wide shots, street atmosphere, team setups

For golden hour positioning: find a race car with Pikes Peak clearly visible behind it from the west side of the display area. Position yourself so the mountain fills the upper background frame behind the car. At 7:45 PM, everything in the frame is bathed in warm amber light. Shoot this for the full 36-minute window — the light changes continuously and each 5-minute increment produces a visibly different photograph.

**Strangers project at Fan Fest:** *"I'm a photographer on a road trip doing a portrait series — one stranger at every landmark I visit. ## SATURDAY, JUNE 20 — GREAT SAND DUNES SUNRISE + ZAPATA FALLS → DENVER AIRPORT

### Day Overview
The last morning. The Great Sand Dunes are the tallest dunes in North America — up to 750 feet high — deposited in the San Luis Valley between two mountain ranges. The sunrise here is unlike every other location on the trip: the Sangre de Cristo Mountains rise directly behind the dunes to the east, which means the peaks catch alpenglow before the sun itself rises over them. You photograph lit mountain peaks above dark dunes in the pre-dawn window, then watch the first raking sunlight sweep across the dune surface at 5:38 AM.

You slept in the parking lot. You walk to your position. This is the same principle as Maroon Bells — no pre-dawn driving panic, no arriving late, no rushing. You are already there.

After the dune session, a quick 9-mile drive south leads to Zapata Falls — a 40-foot waterfall hidden inside a narrow granite canyon, at full June snowmelt volume. 45 minutes there, then the 3.5-hour drive north on I-25 to Denver for the 8:46 PM flight.

Need to be at DEN by 6:45 PM. Leave the dunes by 11:00 AM.

### Saturday Photography Detail

**5:38 AM — Great Sand Dunes Sunrise**
Entry: $35/vehicle or America the Beautiful Annual Pass. You slept in the lot. The walk from the parking area to the first significant dune ridge is 10 minutes across flat sandy terrain. Be on a ridge by 5:15 AM to catch the full pre-dawn sequence.

*Pre-dawn (5:00–5:38 AM):*
The Sangre de Cristo Mountains sit directly east of the dunes. As the sky brightens, the mountain peaks behind catch alpenglow — pink and rose above dark blue — while the dune crests in front remain in deep shadow. This mountain-lit-above-dark-dunes contrast is only available in the 15–20 minutes before sunrise. Shoot it actively.

*Sunrise (5:38 AM):*
First direct light arrives from the east and rakes across the dune surface at an extreme low angle. Every wind ripple in the sand casts a shadow. Every dune crest becomes a sharp luminance transition — bright lit face against dark shadow valley. The graphic quality of the raking light is strongest in the first 30 minutes (5:38–6:10 AM). After 6:10 AM, the sun rises higher, the angle softens, and the shadows shorten.

**What to shoot:**
- **Dune ridge graphic** — Camera 3–6 inches from the sand surface, wide angle (16–24mm), shooting along a dune crest line into the light. Shadow on one side, bright face on the other. The most powerful minimalist image from the dunes.
- **Scale silhouette** — Position yourself high on a ridge in the dark before sunrise. From far below on the flat, photograph your silhouette against the brightening sky on a 750-ft dune crest. Self-timer or remote trigger. The scale of the dune only reads when compared to a human figure.
- **Wind ripple texture close-up** — Close focus on the sand surface at ground level in raking first light. The wind patterns are perfect geometric forms in these conditions. 1/125s, f/11.
- **Sangre de Cristo + dunes** — Wide angle facing east from the dune base. The flat sandy foreground, dune walls in the middle distance, and the 14,000-ft mountain peaks rising directly behind — three completely different landscape elements stacked in one frame.
- **Medano Creek** (if flowing) — The seasonal stream runs along the dune base in late spring and early June from snowmelt. In mid-June, flow may be diminishing but can still be present. If active: wade in and shoot flowing water in foreground, dune walls in mid-ground, mountains behind. Three landscape types in a single frame.

**Camera settings at the dunes:**
- Pre-dawn: tripod essential, ISO 400–800, f/8, 4–15 seconds
- First light: ISO 100, f/8–f/11, 1/125–1/500s depending on exposure
- Wind ripple detail: ISO 100, f/11, 1/125s
- Protect rear lens element from sand during any lens changes — the dunes create their own wind

**9:00 AM — Zapata Falls**
9 miles south of the Great Sand Dunes entrance on CO-150, then a short drive up a dirt access road to the trailhead. A 0.8-mile flat walk leads into a narrow granite slot canyon. The waterfall drops 40 feet through a 10–15 foot wide granite slot at full June snowmelt volume — the water is compressed and forceful. The canyon walls are only arm-span apart at the falls themselves.

- Shoot at 1/30–1/60s for flowing water texture and motion blur — the waterfall silk effect. Tripod or brace against the canyon wall.
- 1/500s to freeze individual water droplets in the spray cloud
- The spray radius is significant at full flow — bring a rain cover or microfiber cloth for the front element
- Wide angle is the only viable focal length given the confined canyon width
