## 🏔️ IS GUADALUPE PEAK A GOOD IDEA? Yes, with conditions

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| Stats ⚠️ | ~8.4 mi round trip · ~3,000 ft gain · NPS estimate 6–8 h · summit 8,751 ft (highest point in Texas) |
| Why it's worth it | You look **down** on El Capitan's crown. The steel summit pyramid makes a good foreground. Views across the Salt Basin dunes and into New Mexico |
| Why it works here | You sleep at the trailhead, start right after sunrise, are back by mid-afternoon, and get a nap before the El Capitan sunset |
| Main risk: **wind** | Check the NWS El Paso forecast Thu. **Rule of thumb:** if gusts above ~50 mph are forecast, swap Friday for **Devil's Hall** (~4.2 mi, flat, sheltered limestone slot ⚠️) |
| Turnaround rule | Summit by **11:30 AM** or turn around |
| Carry | 3–4 L water, windproof shell, gloves, beanie, trekking poles, headlamp |

## SATURDAY, NOV 14 — ⭐ ESCARPMENT SUNRISE → 🍂 McKITTRICK CANYON → CARLSBAD CAVERNS → ⭐⭐ EL CAPITAN WEST-FACE SUNSET → EL PASO NIGHT

**Routing logic:** McKittrick first, because its gate opens around 8 AM and the canyon looks best in morning light. Carlsbad goes in the middle of the day, since a cave doesn't depend on light. You then drive **west past the Guadalupes** to the Salt Flat side for sunset: El Capitan's **west face**, directly on the way to El Paso. Tonight is your only night in El Paso, so both cantina-era classics happen here.

### What to Capture
- **McKittrick:** backlit maples and bigtooth maples against pale limestone, creek reflections. Use a polarizer. If the leaves are down, the canyon walls and stream still carry the hike.
- **Natural Entrance / Big Room:** ISO 3200–6400, f/2.8–f/4. Brace on the rails, no flash. Fix the warm white balance in RAW.
- **⭐⭐ Salt Flat sunset:** 70–200mm for El Capitan + Guadalupe Peak stacked above the white flats; 16–35mm low to the ground for desert texture.

## SUNDAY, NOV 15 — ⭐ SAN AUGUSTIN PASS SUNRISE → 🌄 ORGAN MOUNTAINS → WHITE SANDS → ⭐⭐ DUNE SUNSET → NIGHT IN THE DUNES

### Day Overview
**Routing logic:** to give White Sands both golden hours, you **sleep in the dunes** on a backcountry permit, which moves the Organ Mountains to the morning. That ordering works: from San Augustin Pass the Needles sit almost due south, and the rising sun hits them from the side at close to 90°. Side light is what brings out the spires' texture. La Cueva is a few minutes off the same road, then White Sands from late morning, a long nap, and **sunset on the dunes**.

**Driving:** ~145 mi. **Alarm: 5:50 AM (Mon).**

**Missile-test closure on US-70?** Approach via El Paso → US-54 → Alamogordo, and do the Organs on Monday morning on the way back.

### What to Capture
- **Pass sunrise:** 70–200mm to stack basin layers; turn south for the side-lit Needles. Include the road curve for scale.
- **La Cueva:** the rock shelter framed by desert scrub; the Organs' west side stays in soft shadow, which suits a 24–70mm.
- **⭐⭐ Dune sunset:** camera low and wide on the ripples; a lone soaptree yucca as the only vertical; 70–200mm to compress dune crests. Stay **15 min after sunset** for the pink afterglow on the white sand.
