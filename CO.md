### TUESDAY PHOTOGRAPHY

**5:31 AM — Many Parks Curve**
Entry $35 or Annual Pass. Multiple glacially carved mountain parks (alpine meadows) spread across the valley below at different elevations — each ridge a different luminance value from warm orange foreground to cool blue distance. This layered tonal depth is the shot.
- **70–200mm** — compress ridgelines into stacked layers, 4–5 receding into the frame
- **16–24mm** — full panorama with foreground tundra and road curving into the frame
- Temperature at 9,600 ft in June can be 35–45°F. Bring a packable down jacket.

**Forest Canyon Overlook** — 2,500-ft U-shaped glacial canyon below. Telephoto for the canyon floor river. Wide for the full canyon width.

**Alpine Visitor Center tundra** — At 11,796 ft, the tundra has alpine wildflowers in early June against remnant snow. Low-angle macro shots of tiny blooms against white snow — a frame unique to this elevation.

**Milner Pass** — Continental Divide marker at the summit. Simple graphic: the sign against blue sky, two sides of a continent draining away.

**Twin Lakes** — Two glacial lakes reflecting Mount Elbert (14,440 ft, Colorado's highest) and Mount Massive. Midday — shoot the reflection regardless of light angle. 45 minutes maximum.

**Independence Pass** — One of the highest paved roads in North America. Shoot the road itself curving through the alpine terrain — the tarmac ribbon is the primary subject, not just the peaks behind it.

**5:25–8:30 PM — Maroon Bells evening scout**
The bell-shaped peaks are northwest-facing — they catch warm afternoon and evening light. Tonight is dual purpose: shoot the golden hour light on the peaks AND find your exact sunrise position at Maroon Lake. Walk to the northeast end of the lake, identify the angle where both peaks reflect simultaneously, mark your tripod position. Sleep at the pullout 7 minutes from your sunrise shot.

### WEDNESDAY PHOTOGRAPHY

**5:33 AM — Maroon Bells Lake Reflection**
*Timed entry required after 8 AM. Self-drive before 8 AM — no reservation needed.*

You slept 7 minutes away. You walk to a pre-scouted position in the dark. This is the core advantage of car sleeping: the most photographed image in Colorado and you are already there.

The Maroon Bells are two 14,000-ft peaks with snow on the upper slopes in June, green aspen shoreline, and still water below. The reflection window is 4:45–6:30 AM before wind develops. At 5:33 AM, first light hits the upper spires while the lake is still in cool blue shadow — shoot this warm/cool contrast continuously.

- **16–24mm** — full reflection, both peaks, aspen framing both sides
- **50mm** — cleaner sky-to-reflection ratio
- **70–200mm** — peak isolation, snow detail in upper couloirs
- Shoot RAW — the dynamic range exceeds JPEG

**CO-82 Aspen Valley Road (8:00–8:45 AM)** — Morning sidelight on the valley corridor. Castle Peak and the Elk Mountains to the south. Drive slowly, pull over at any composition that reads. 45 minutes of road photography unique to this valley.

**Curecanti NRA / Blue Mesa Reservoir** — Colorado's largest reservoir directly on US-50. Zero detour. Pull over at any of the overlooks. Deep blue water against pale canyon walls — 15 minutes.

**Black Canyon of the Gunnison** — Entry $35 or Annual Pass.

*Gunnison Point* — Primary overlook. Canyon drops 2,722 ft to the Gunnison River. Wide angle for the full drop. Telephoto to compress the canyon layers — the darkness of the 1.8-billion-year-old Precambrian gneiss against the pale sky gives the canyon visual weight unlike any other.

*Painted Wall* — Colorado's tallest cliff face at 2,250 ft. White and pink pegmatite veins streak through the dark rock face like brushstrokes. Telephoto to bring in vein detail across the void.

*Chasm View sunset (6:00–8:30 PM)* — The canyon runs roughly east-west. At sunset, light rakes across the southern wall face. The canyon shadows deepen from dark brown to near-black while the rim rocks above hold warm orange light. At peak golden hour (7:45–8:20 PM), the lit rim against the black canyon void is the frame. After sunset (8:20–8:30 PM): canyon in deep blue, sky in warm gradient — stay for this 10-minute window.

### THURSDAY PHOTOGRAPHY

**5:35 AM — Ouray Mountain Bowl**
Ouray sits in a natural amphitheater at 7,792 ft. The Amphitheater area south of town or the Perimeter Trail overlook gives the full bowl view. At sunrise, upper peaks catch warm alpenglow while the town below remains in cool shadow — the contrast of illuminated peaks against the dark valley communicates the geography perfectly.

**7:15 AM — Blue Lakes Hike**
*Most demanding hike of the trip. Budget 3.5–4 hours.* Dallas Creek Road (dirt, standard vehicles fine) to the trailhead. 1,800 ft gain over 2.75 miles to the lower lake, another 600 ft to the upper lakes at ~12,000 ft. In mid-June: snow and ice on lake edges, turquoise glacially-fed water beneath, Mount Sneffels (14,158 ft) and the Sneffels Wilderness behind. This is the most saturated color photograph of the trip — the blue-green water against remnant white snow and dark gray rock.

- Wide angle from the shoreline — foreground rock and snow at water's edge for depth
- Telephoto — compress Mount Sneffels against the lake surface
- **Altitude note:** You came from Austin (620 ft). You are now at 12,000 ft on Day 4. Hydrate aggressively. If any headache or nausea develops, descend.

**Bear Creek Falls** — US-550, 3 miles south of Ouray. A roadside pullout, 10-minute stop. The falls pour off the canyon wall directly beside the highway at full June snowmelt volume.

**Red Mountain Pass — Midday and Golden Hour**
The three Red Mountains flanking the pass have iron oxide in the rock that gives them vivid rust-orange and deep red color. This is not brown or grey — it is genuinely red, and in good light it reads as almost unnaturally saturated. The pass sits at 11,018 ft on US-550 between Ouray (north) and Silverton (south).

At midday (11:50 AM): scout golden hour positions. The pullouts on the south side of the pass looking north toward the red peaks with the highway curving away toward Silverton is the primary composition.

At golden hour (4:55–8:30 PM): 3.5 hours at the pass. The iron-oxide peaks shift through a color sequence from warm orange at 7:45 PM through deep crimson-red to a final purple-red before dark. At peak (8:00–8:15 PM) the color intensity is extreme — the reddest mountains you will see anywhere in Colorado against a late June sky.

- Highway leading south toward Silverton with red peaks behind — leading line composition
- Rust peaks against saturated blue sky — wide angle, polarizer if available
- Telephoto on the oxidized cliff face texture

**Silverton (12:40–4:30 PM)**
A preserved 1880s silver mining town at 9,318 ft surrounded by 13,000-ft peaks on all sides. The entire downtown is on the National Register of Historic Places. Blair Street (historic red-light district) has intact Victorian storefronts. Greene Street is the main commercial strip. Shoot the old town architecture in afternoon sidelight — weathered wood and painted metal signs in 1–3 PM light.

### FRIDAY PHOTOGRAPHY

**5:15–6:00 AM — Durango Blue Hour + D&SNG Steam Locomotive**
You slept in the depot lot. Walk out of your car into your photography location.

Durango's Main Avenue is lined with intact 1880s Victorian commercial buildings — ornate brick facades, cast-iron details, original storefronts. At blue hour (5:15–5:45 AM) the street lamps are still lit against a deep blue sky. The combination of warm building light and cold blue exterior creates a natural color contrast that disappears at sunrise.

- Wide angle down Main Avenue — buildings receding with mountain silhouette at the end of the street
- Storefront window reflections — the blue morning sky in plate glass
- D&SNG Depot (479 Main Ave) — the 1881 Victorian depot building and train shed. Engine house activity begins early. Even before the trains move, the iron locomotive structure against the pre-dawn sky is a strong frame. Telephoto for boiler and wheel detail.

**6:45–11:00 AM — Mesa Verde National Park**
Entry: $35 or Annual Pass. Cliff Palace ranger tour: $5, booked on Recreation.gov.

Every other location on this trip is geological — canyons, mountains, sandstone, dunes. Mesa Verde introduces architecture from 700–900 years ago carved directly into the canyon walls. Cliff Palace is a 150-room dwelling built between 1190–1270 CE, set in a south-facing natural alcove that receives direct morning-to-afternoon light. In June, the alcove face is illuminated from the east by 8 AM.

*Sun Point Overlook (7:20–8:00 AM)*
Faces south and east across the Montezuma Valley with the San Juan Mountains on the horizon. In morning light, the canyon system catches warm directional light. The landscape shot of Mesa Verde — cultural elements small in the canyon below, dwarfed by geological scale. Wide angle for the full panorama.

*Cliff Palace (8:00–9:30 AM)*
The south-facing alcove receives direct morning light from the east. The white and tan plaster walls, kiva openings, and tower structures are illuminated in warm directional light.
- **From the overlook** (no tour required): Telephoto (200mm+) brings the dwellings forward across the canyon. The dwelling is ~100 meters across — this is the clean composition without tourists in the foreground.
- **On the ranger tour**: You descend into the canyon and stand at the base of the dwelling. The four-story tower fills the frame from below. Wide angle looking up captures the full dwelling height against the sandstone alcove ceiling. This composition is only possible on the tour.

*Spruce Canyon Overlook and Chapin Mesa loop (9:30–10:30 AM)*
Multiple additional dwelling sites visible from the mesa-top road. Drive the 6-mile archaeological loop hitting the pullouts. Canyon views with dwelling structures in the walls continue throughout.

*Cathedral Spires* — Three vertical formations. Wide angle from below exaggerates height.

### SATURDAY PHOTOGRAPHY

**5:38 AM — Great Sand Dunes Sunrise**
Entry: $35 or Annual Pass. You slept in the parking lot. Walk in, already there.

The Sangre de Cristo Mountains sit directly east of the dunes. Pre-dawn (5:00–5:38 AM): the mountains behind catch alpenglow while the dune crests in front remain in deep blue shadow. Shoot this mountain-lit-above-dark-dunes contrast before sunrise.

At 5:38 AM: first direct light arrives from the east and rakes across the dune surface at an extreme low angle. Every wind ripple throws a shadow. The dune ridges become sharp luminance contrasts — dark shadow valleys against bright lit crests. This is the photography window and it lasts 30–40 minutes before the sun rises higher and the raking angle softens.

- **Dune ridge graphic** — Camera 3–6 inches from the sand, wide angle, shooting along a ridge into the light. Shadow on one side, light on the other. The minimalist dune shot.
- **Scale silhouette** — Position yourself on a distant ridge before sunrise. From the base, shoot your silhouette on the 750-ft dune crest against the brightening sky. Self-timer or remote.
- **Wind ripple texture** — Close focus on ripple patterns in the sand surface in raking first light. Perfect geometric forms at 1/125s, f/11.
- **Medano Creek** — If the seasonal stream is still flowing in mid-June, it runs along the dune base. Turquoise flowing water + towering sand dunes + 14,000-ft peaks — three completely different landscapes in one frame. Check current flow on the park website before arriving.

**9:00 AM — Zapata Falls**
9 miles south on CO-150, then a short drive up a dirt road. 0.8-mile flat walk into a narrow granite slot canyon. A 40-ft waterfall hidden in the canyon at full June snowmelt volume — the water is confined to a 10-ft-wide slot with significant force and spray. Bring a rain cover for your lens. Shoot at 1/30–1/60s for flowing water texture, f/11.
