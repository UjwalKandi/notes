## FRIDAY, AUG 14 — COLUMBIA GORGE WATERFALL CORRIDOR → PORTLAND CITY DAY → CANNON BEACH SUNSET

### Day Overview
The richest single day for variety on the whole trip — waterfalls, a weird-and-wonderful city, and a beach sunset, in that order. You start at first light on the Historic Columbia River Highway, the 1916 scenic road that strings the Gorge's waterfalls like beads. From Chanticleer Point you catch sunrise over the Gorge, then descend through a chain of falls — Latourell, Bridal Veil, Wahkeena, Multnomah, Horsetail, Ponytail — timed to hit Multnomah before 9 AM (no permit needed pre-9, no crowds). North-facing shaded canyons give soft, even light on all of them, the ideal window.

By late morning you drop into Portland for a full "Keep Portland Weird" city day — brunch, the largest bookstore on Earth, an oddities shop, a minimalist Japanese design store, oyster stew at a 1907 institution, local ice cream. This is the one true city block of the trip; lean into it. Then, instead of an early coast dash, you hold Portland until ~6:15 PM and drive US-26 west to arrive at Cannon Beach for the main event: **Haystack Rock silhouetted against the sunset** — the classic backlit Oregon sea-stack shot. You sleep at Cannon Beach, ten minutes from tomorrow's Ecola sunrise.

## SATURDAY, AUG 15 — ECOLA CLIFF SUNRISE → OREGON COAST (TIERED) → BOARDMAN SUNSET

### Day Overview
The length of the Oregon coast — Cannon Beach to Brookings is ~340 miles of US-101 — so this is the discipline day. You open at Ecola State Park's cliff at sunrise, warm light raking the headlands as you look down onto Haystack and Indian Beach. From there you thread south hitting the coast's greatest hits, but you cannot do all of them — the drive alone eats 7 hours, so the plan is tiered. Three fixed anchors: **Ecola** (sunrise), **Thor's Well** at Cape Perpetua (timed to high tide — genuinely dangerous, telephoto from a safe distance, never turn your back on the sea), and the **Samuel Boardman Corridor** at sunset (the 10/10 coast image — sea stacks and natural arches backlit in gold).

Between the anchors, pick 2–3 optionals by tide and pace: the wild capes (Neahkahnie, Oswald West), the lighthouses (Yaquina Head, Heceta Head + Sea Lion Caves), or the Florence dunes. The Three Capes detour and God's Thumb are skip-unless-far-ahead time-sinks. Lunch is dock-to-plate chowder at Yachats. You sleep at Brookings, positioned for Secret Beach at dawn.

## SUNDAY, AUG 16 — SECRET BEACH SUNRISE → NORTHERN REDWOODS (TRIMMED) → CRATER LAKE SUNSET + PERSEIDS

### Day Overview
Three different worlds in one day: hidden-cove Pacific coast at dawn, the tallest trees on Earth through midday, and a volcanic caldera under a meteor shower at night. You open at Secret Beach in the Boardman Corridor — a stream crossing the sand toward offshore stacks, soft with August fog. Then you drop into California's northern redwoods.

**This is the trimmed version — deliberately.** To protect a *scouted* Crater Lake sunset (rather than screeching in right at the light), the redwood loop is cut to its three strongest, most efficient stops: Battery Point, Stout Grove + Howland Hill, and Fern Canyon. The 🟡 stops (Lady Bird Johnson Grove, Elk Meadow/Trillium, False Klamath Cove, Redwood Creek Beach) are dropped to buy ~2 hours — which puts you on the Crater Lake rim with 30–45 min of scouting light before the 10/10 sunset. Old-growth photographs best at midday anyway (canopy diffuses harsh sun), so the middle of the day here is a feature, not a waste. Then the long climb inland via US-199 and OR-62 to Crater Lake for the caldera sunset from the west rim and the astro centerpiece of the whole trip — Milky Way + Perseids over the lake under a new-moon sky. You sleep on the rim.

### Redwoods — What to Cover (trimmed core, in sequence)
1. **Battery Point Lighthouse** — 1856 light on a tidal islet; walk the causeway only at low tide, else telephoto from shore.
2. **Stout Grove** — the most cathedral-like grove; flat alluvial floor, evenly spaced giants. 16–35mm vertical up the trunks; god-rays if fog + sun combine.
3. **Howland Hill Road** — unpaved 10-mi lane *through* the old-growth (your SUV handles it); the road framed by trunks is the shot. Connects straight from Stout Grove.
4. **Fern Canyon** — 30–80 ft walls solid with ferns, stream on the floor (Jurassic Park II site). Waterproof footwear; slow shutter on the water; wide for the wall texture.

### Crater Lake — Sunday Evening (West Rim) Sequence
Sinnott Memorial (Rim Village) for orientation → Merriam / Discovery Point → **Watchman Overlook** for sunset (west rim looks *east* across the lake, so the far wall + Wizard Island catch the last warm light) → hold for astro with a clear southern horizon and Wizard Island in frame (galactic core sits south). Polarizer for the daytime blue; 14–24mm f/2.8 for the Milky Way.

## MONDAY, AUG 17 — CRATER LAKE SUNRISE → VOLCANIC LEGACY BYWAY → LASSEN MAIN HIGHWAY → SF RED-EYE

### Day Overview
The monster day, where your low-sleep endurance earns its keep — two Cascade volcanoes bookend a single southbound line, with Mt. Shasta and a spring-fed waterfall in between. You open with a caldera sunrise from the east rim: because sunrise lights the *west* wall, Cloudcap Overlook (highest paved point, 8,065 ft) gives you the warm-lit far wall while you pick off a few more Crater viewpoints before exiting south.

Then you descend the Volcanic Legacy Scenic Byway (OR-62 → US-97 → CA-89), the road built to string the Cascade volcanoes together. Mt. Shasta dominates the horizon for an hour — grab a small-town diner plate at Weed. Burney Falls is next, a 129-ft curtain fed by springs pouring straight out of the rock face. Then into Lassen from the north (Manzanita Lake) for a clean one-way traverse of the park highway — the ideal first-timer route, showing the park's full range in a single line: a reflection lake, the volcano head-on, two intensely colored alpine lakes, the signature geothermal basin at Bumpass Hell, and roadside steam at Sulphur Works on the way out south.

The accepted trade: Monday's sunset is spent on I-5 to SFO — unavoidable with Lassen, and the right call since SF is flight-only. You fuel and grab cash in Redding, eat cheap and local in the city, drop the car, and sleep on the 12:45 AM red-eye — at your desk by 9:22 AM with zero pay lost.

### Crater Lake — Monday Morning (East Rim) Sequence
Sunrise lights the west wall, so shoot from the east/southeast rim: **Cloudcap Overlook** (highest paved, 8,065 ft) → **Phantom Ship Overlook** (jagged ghost-ship islet) → **Sun Notch** (0.8-mi walk to a dramatic Phantom Ship framing) → **Pumice Castle Overlook** (orange pinnacle in the wall) → exit south. *If ahead:* Vidae Falls + Pinnacles Overlook near the south exit.

### Lassen — What to Cover, In Sequence (north → south)
1. **Manzanita Lake** — Lassen Peak (10,457 ft) reflected in calm water; the classic. 16–24mm, foreground reeds. Calmest early.
2. **Lassen Peak Overlook** — the volcano head-on (last erupted 1914–17).
3. **Emerald Lake** — small, intense blue-green alpine lake. Polarizer.
4. **Lake Helen** — deep cobalt glacial lake, one of the clearest in the park. Polarizer for saturation.
5. **Bumpass Hell** — 3-mi RT boardwalk into the largest hydrothermal basin: boiling mudpots, fumaroles, turquoise-and-milky pools. Stay on the boardwalk (thin crust, scalding). Wide for the basin; 70–200mm for the pools + steam. Lassen's signature alien-terrain frame.
6. **Sulphur Works** — roadside steam vents by the south entrance; final geothermal grab.

### What to Capture — Byway Stops
- **Mt. Shasta (Weed):** 14,179-ft stratovolcano rising alone. 24–70mm with foreground fields for scale; 70–200mm to compress the summit. ISO 100, f/8, polarizer for haze.
- **Burney Falls:** full curtain from the lower viewpoint — water emerges from the rock face itself, so the whole cliff "weeps." Tripod/ND, 1/4–1/30s. Shaded gorge holds up at midday.
