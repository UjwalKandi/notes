### ⟡ OREGON CAVES — THE GO/NO-GO RULE

**Do it only if you leave Secret Beach by 7:15 AM and aren't fighting weather delays.** Here's the math: Oregon Caves is a 40-mile round-trip detour off US-199 (OR-46 is a slow, winding 20 miles each way, ~45 min per direction) plus a ~90-minute ranger-led tour. Total cost: ~2.5 hours. Your Sunday has a comfortable midday buffer (the drive to Crater Lake only needs to have you on the rim by ~5 PM for the Watchman session), so the detour fits **if the coast sunrise and drive run on schedule.**

**What you get:** The "Marble Halls of Oregon" — a rare marble cave (most US caves are limestone), ranger-guided through narrow passages and formations. Above ground, the monument protects one of the few remaining old-growth groves in the area with a giant Douglas fir. Entry to the monument is free; the cave tour is ~$10, first-come or limited reservation — check nps.gov/orca for the day's tour times.

**Photography note:** Cave interiors are dark and tripods are generally restricted on tours — this is an *experience* stop more than a portfolio stop. The above-ground old-growth trail and the historic 1930s Oregon Caves Chateau are the better photo subjects. Treat this as a "because I have the time and it's rare" stop, not a golden-hour play.

**Skip it if:** the coast sunrise runs long, weather slows the drive, or you'd rather bank the buffer for a relaxed Crater Lake afternoon. Nothing downstream depends on it.

### Sunday Photography Detail

**6:00 AM — Secret Beach** — A short trail to a cove where a stream crosses the beach toward offshore sea stacks. Stream as foreground leading line, stacks anchoring the horizon. East-facing cove, soft dawn light, August fog adds mood.

**Crater Lake** — Entry via Annual Pass. Watchman Overlook (west rim) looks across to Wizard Island. Sunset at 7:45 shifts the lake cobalt to indigo. 16–24mm for full caldera, 70–200mm to isolate Wizard Island; polarizer dialed carefully.

**★ 9:30 PM — Milky Way + Perseids** — New moon + one of the darkest skies in the Lower 48 + active Perseids. Galactic core sits south over the caldera. 14–24mm f/2.8, ISO 3200–6400, 15–20s. Intervalometer running continuous 20s frames for meteors. Red headlamp only; the rim drops off steeply.
