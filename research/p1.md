# Phase 1 — Detailed Implementation Plan
**Window:** June 1 – June 28, 2026 (4 weeks)
**Phase Goal:** End June with locked decisions on (a) 2 research areas, (b) 12 programs, (c) 25 target professors, (d) a 1-page Research Vision, and (e) a stubbed research website.

> **Why Phase 1 matters more than every other phase:** Every later phase compounds on these decisions. Wrong research area → wrong programs → wrong professors → wrong SoP → rejections. Spending 4 weeks on direction is not "slow" — it's the cheapest insurance you'll buy this year.

---

## Time Budget (Be Realistic With Yourself)

You have a full-time Apple/Welo job. Allocate:
- **Weekday evenings:** 1.5 hrs × 4 nights = 6 hrs/week
- **Saturdays:** 5 hrs (deep work — papers, writing)
- **Sundays:** 4 hrs (planning, outreach prep, light reading)
- **Total:** ~15 hrs/week → **~60 hrs across June**

If you can't protect 15 hrs/week starting next week, you need to either (a) push the cycle to 2027, or (b) talk to your manager about reduced project intensity through December. Don't pretend the time will appear.

**Sacred rules for this phase:**
- No application-writing yet. None. SoPs in June = wasted SoPs.
- No emailing professors yet. (You'd email the wrong ones.)
- One ritual: every Sunday evening, 30 min review of the week + plan the next.

### WEEK 1 (Jun 1 – Jun 7) — *Map the Field*

**Theme:** Get the lay of the land. Read enough to know what you don't know.

**Daily breakdown:**

**Mon–Tue (3 hrs total):** Set up infrastructure
- [ ] Create Google Sheet from §11 of Master Plan (5 tabs)
- [ ] Create a Notion or Obsidian vault: `PhD-Prep/` with subfolders: `Papers/`, `Programs/`, `Professors/`, `Drafts/`, `Outreach/`
- [ ] Install **Zotero** (free) — your paper library. Add the browser extension.
- [ ] Set up Google Scholar alerts for these queries (gives you fresh papers weekly):
  - `"LLM observability"`
  - `"agent reliability" OR "agent monitoring"`
  - `"data quality" "machine learning" pipelines`
  - `"ML systems" benchmark`
  - `"information systems" "artificial intelligence" research`

**Wed–Fri (4.5 hrs total):** Read 5 *survey* papers — surveys, not technical papers. They orient you fastest.
Suggested starter set (find via Google Scholar; pick most-cited recent ones):
- A survey on ML systems / MLOps
- A survey on LLM agents / agentic AI
- A survey on data-centric AI
- A recent paper from Berkeley RISE / Sky Lab on AI infrastructure
- A recent IS-track paper on AI in organizations (MIS Quarterly or ISR)

For each: **5-bullet note** in Obsidian — (1) problem, (2) approach, (3) what's still open, (4) does this excite me?, (5) which professor wrote it.

**Sat (5 hrs):** Deep field-mapping session
- [ ] On a single page, sketch 4 quadrants: **Systems for AI** | **AI for Systems** | **Data for AI** | **Human-AI Interaction**
- [ ] Drop every paper you've read into a quadrant
- [ ] Mark the ones that *electrified* you with a star
- [ ] Notice which quadrant has the most stars — that's your direction signal

**Sun (4 hrs):** Synthesize + plan Week 2
- [ ] Write a 1-page "field map" — what subfields exist, who works in them, what venues publish them
- [ ] Pick **3 candidate research areas** to investigate further next week (you'll narrow to 2 later)
- [ ] Block calendar for Week 2

**Week 1 Deliverables:**
1. ✅ Tracking spreadsheet live
2. ✅ Zotero with 5+ papers
3. ✅ 5 paper notes
4. ✅ 1-page field map
5. ✅ 3 candidate research areas

### WEEK 2 (Jun 8 – Jun 14) — *Find the People*

**Theme:** For each candidate research area, identify the 8–10 most influential professors at your target schools.

**Where to find professors:**

| Source | Use |
|---|---|
| **CSRankings.org** | Filter by area (AI, Systems, Data Mgmt, HCI) and school. Shows publication-weighted rankings of faculty. **Best single tool for CS PhD targeting.** |
| **Google Scholar** | Search faculty pages → see citation counts and recent papers |
| **Lab websites** | Most labs have a "people" page with current students |
| **Recent conference proceedings** | NeurIPS, MLSys, SIGMOD, OSDI, CHI 2024–2026 papers — note the senior author affiliations |
| **OpenReview.net** | Filter by area chair / reviewer affiliations |
| **Twitter/X academic CS community** | Follow professors; see what they're working on |
| **For IS PhDs:** | INFORMS, MIS Quarterly, ISR recent papers — different community than CS |

**Daily breakdown:**

**Mon–Tue (3 hrs):** Use CSRankings to pull a long list
- [ ] For each of your 3 candidate areas (e.g., MLSys, Data Management for ML, HCI/Agents), filter CSRankings to your 12 target schools
- [ ] Extract names of top 5–10 professors per school per area into the **Faculty tab** of your spreadsheet
- [ ] Goal: **~50 names** at this stage (you'll cut to 25 next week)

**Wed–Fri (4.5 hrs):** For each professor, do a quick "**5-minute screen**":
- [ ] Read titles of their last 5 papers (Google Scholar profile)
- [ ] Glance at their lab website
- [ ] Note in spreadsheet:
  - Lab name
  - Currently advising? (rough estimate from website)
  - Are they tenured/full-prof, associate, or assistant? (assistant profs often take more students; full profs often have grant money but might be "famous and busy")
  - Style: theory-heavy or systems-builder? (Read paper titles — "On the Theory of..." vs. "A System for...")
  - **Fit score 1–5** (gut feel — does this person work on what excites you?)

**Sat (5 hrs):** Deep dive on top 15
- [ ] Take the 15 highest fit-score profs
- [ ] For each, read **one full paper** (skim is fine — focus on intro + conclusion + one figure)
- [ ] Write a 3-sentence "why I want to work with X" for each — even if rough
- [ ] If you can't write 3 honest sentences, drop that prof

**Sun (4 hrs):** Refine the list
- [ ] Cull from 50 → 30
- [ ] For each remaining, find **2–3 of their current PhD students** (from lab websites). Add to "PhD Students" tab
- [ ] Identify 2–3 *recent grads* who are now in industry/postdocs — these are great to email; they have time and remember the application game

**Week 2 Deliverables:**
1. ✅ 30 professors mapped with fit scores
2. ✅ 15 read at depth-1
3. ✅ 30+ current PhD students identified
4. ✅ Email format prepared (don't send yet)

### WEEK 3 (Jun 15 – Jun 21) — *Narrow & Pressure-Test*

**Theme:** From 30 professors and 3 areas, narrow to 25 professors and 2 areas. Stress-test your direction with real conversations.

**Daily breakdown:**

**Mon–Tue (3 hrs):** Cluster analysis
- [ ] Group the 30 profs by research area. Patterns will emerge.
- [ ] If you have 18 profs in "Systems for AI" and only 5 in "AI for Networks," that's data — follow it.
- [ ] **Decision:** Pick your 2 final research areas (one primary, one secondary that could be the same SoP angle)

> **Heuristic:** Your two areas should share ~60% overlap in language. E.g., "MLSys + Data Management for ML" share talks about pipelines, distributed systems, data quality. "AI Safety + Computer Networks" do not. Coherent narrative > breadth.

**Wed–Thu (3 hrs):** Build the 12-program target list
For each program, verify in spreadsheet:
- [ ] Confirm at least 2 faculty from your prof list are at that program
- [ ] Note the deadline (varies Dec 1 – Dec 15 typically; some Nov 30; some IS programs have Jan 5)
- [ ] Note application fee
- [ ] Note GRE/TOEFL requirement (check **2026–27 admissions page**, not last year's)
- [ ] Note required documents (some need writing samples; UC schools require Personal History Statement)
- [ ] Star "Apply Early" programs (some have Nov 15 priority deadlines)

**Fri (1.5 hrs):** First reach-out — but to PhD students, not professors yet
- [ ] Email 5 current PhD students. Template:

> **Subject:** Question from a Fall 2027 applicant — your work on [topic]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm a 4-yr industry data/AI infrastructure engineer (Apple via Welo, formerly Stronghold AI capstone) considering applying to PhD programs this fall. I came across your work on [specific project] in Prof. [advisor]'s lab.
>
> Would you be open to a 20-min chat in the next 2 weeks? I'd love to learn what the day-to-day is like, what you wish you'd known when applying, and whether my background sounds like a fit for the lab. Happy to send specific questions in advance.
>
> Either way, thanks for your time — and your work on [project] is exactly why I'm interested in this area.
>
> Ujwal Kandi
> [linkedin] | [github]

> **Why students, not faculty, this week:** Students will reply. Faculty in June are slammed with NSF grants and grading. Students give you ground-truth: how funded is the lab, what's the advisor's style, who recently graduated, what's the bar for admits. You'll know more after 3 student calls than 30 paper reads.

**Sat (5 hrs):** Take the calls (if any are scheduled) + course-correct
- [ ] If a student call surfaces "this lab isn't taking students next year" or "advisor is moving to industry" — adjust list immediately
- [ ] Update fit scores based on what you heard

**Sun (4 hrs):** Final shortlists + write the **Research Vision v0**
- [ ] Lock the **12 programs** (4 reach / 5 match / 3 safety per Master Plan §3)
- [ ] Lock the **25 professors** (~2 per program)
- [ ] **Draft a 1-page "Research Vision" doc.** Structure:
  - ¶1: One specific research question that grips you (1–2 sentences)
  - ¶2: Why it matters (3–4 sentences) — cite 2 papers
  - ¶3: Why your background uniquely positions you (3 sentences)
  - ¶4: 3 sub-questions you'd want to investigate
  - ¶5: Methods you'd use (systems-building, empirical study, benchmarking, etc.)

This is *not* a finished SoP. It's a thinking document. You'll rewrite it 5+ times. The goal is to *commit to direction*.

**Week 3 Deliverables:**
1. ✅ Final 2 research areas
2. ✅ Final 12 programs (with deadlines & requirements)
3. ✅ Final 25 professors
4. ✅ 5 student outreach emails sent
5. ✅ Research Vision v0 (1 page)

## Risks & Watch-Outs in Phase 1

| Risk | Sign | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Endless reading, no decisions** | "I need to read 10 more papers before deciding" | Hard cut on June 14. Decide on 2 areas with whatever you know. |
| **Falling in love with one professor** | Your whole list is one lab | Force diversification: every program needs ≥2 viable POIs. |
| **Optimizing for prestige, not fit** | Your top picks are all "name brand" | Re-rank by fit score, not school name. Apply with conviction, not envy. |
| **Avoiding the academic conversation** | "I'll email the professor in July, when I'm ready" | You're never ready. Send the coffee email this week. |
| **Industry-resume thinking** | Listing tools/tech in your Research Vision | A research vision is about *questions*, not *technologies*. Rewrite if you're naming Kafka. |
| **Burnout from full-time + this** | Working past 11pm 3+ nights | Take a Saturday off. Sustainability matters; this is a 6-month sprint. |

## What to Do in Parallel (From Later Phases)

Phases are sequential in *focus* but not in *activity*. Here's what you can quietly start in Phase 1 to de-risk later phases — **but only ~3 hrs/week of total parallel work**, or you'll dilute Phase 1.

### From Phase 2 (Research Asset + Networking) → start in Week 3
- **Scope your research artifact mentally.** While reading papers in Weeks 1–2, keep a separate list: *"papers that smell like the gap I could fill."* By end of Week 4, you should have a candidate problem for AIDE-v2.
- **Reach out to 1 industry colleague at Apple/Welo** about whether you could share *anonymized* observability data or framings as a basis for a benchmark. (Don't violate any IP — but a public benchmark inspired by real production patterns is gold for an SoP.)

### From Phase 3 (Application Materials) → start passively in Week 1
- **Keep an "SoP Snippets" file in Obsidian.** Every time you read a paper or have a thought like *"that's exactly the kind of work I want to do because…"* — paste it in. By August 1 you'll have 30+ raw paragraphs. Easier to edit than to write from scratch.
- **Build the academic CV outline in Week 4.** Just headers: Education / Research Experience / Publications & Preprints / Selected Projects / Talks / Awards / Teaching / Service / Skills. Fill it in over July.

### From Phase 5 (Research Output) → start in Week 4
- **Create a private GitHub repo `aide-v2-benchmark`** in Week 4. Write a `README.md` with the *problem statement* even before any code. Future-you will thank present-you.

### From Phase 4 (Tests) → start in Week 2
- **Resolve test logistics this month** so they don't ambush you in September:
  - Audit each program's GRE/TOEFL requirement (column in your spreadsheet)
  - If GRE needed: book a slot for **late August** (slots fill up; book by mid-July)
  - If TOEFL needed: book for late August
  - **Most CS programs have permanently waived GRE.** McCombs IROM and some IS programs still require/recommend it. Stanford GSB requires GRE/GMAT.

### From Phase 6 (LoRs) → start in Week 1, complete in Phase 3
- **Build a "LoR confidence ladder" now.** In your spreadsheet, list every potential LoR writer ranked 1–10 on:
  - Can speak to research ability (not just "good employee")? (×3 weight)
  - Has a name your committee would recognize? (×1)
  - Will *actually* write a strong, customized letter on time? (×2)
  - Have I had >1 substantial conversation with them in past 18 months? (×1)
- The top 4 names become your LoR pipeline. **The Week 4 coffee chat with a UT professor is part of this.** You're not asking yet — you're investing in the relationship.

### What NOT to do in parallel
- ❌ Don't write SoP drafts in June. Your direction will shift; drafts will be wasted.
- ❌ Don't email professors yet. The first email matters; send it from a place of clarity (Phase 2).
- ❌ Don't redesign your industry resume in June. (You'll do an *academic CV* in August, separate doc.)
- ❌ Don't take the GRE in June. Book it; don't sit it.

## Daily Habit (Throughout Phase 1)

**Morning (10 min, before work):** Skim arXiv cs.LG, cs.IR, cs.DB new submissions for your area. Save 1–2 to Zotero. Don't read deeply — just collect.

**Evening, twice a week (20 min):** Read one paper from the queue. Take the 5-bullet note.

**Friday evening (15 min):** Write a "Friday note" — one paragraph in your journal: *what did I learn this week? What am I confused about? What's my next move?*

This habit alone — sustained from June through December — will make you measurably better at "thinking like a researcher" by application time. It shows up in the SoP. Committees can tell.

## One Last Thing — The Mindset Shift

In your industry job, you ship features and fix incidents. **The unit of work is delivery.**

In a PhD, the unit of work is a *question* — asked precisely enough that the answer can be defended for hours. Phase 1 is your training ground for asking precise questions.

Notice how often this month you find yourself saying *"I should build X"* vs. *"I want to understand whether Y is true."* The first is engineering. The second is research. By June 28, you should be saying the second sentence at least as often as the first. If you're not, you have your answer about whether this PhD is right for you — and that's also a win.
