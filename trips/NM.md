Pure positioning, and the reason this trip gets four *full* shooting days instead of three and a half. ## FRIDAY, OCT 30 — DAVIS MTNS SUNRISE → BALMORHEA MIDDAY → ⭐⭐ McDONALD OBSERVATORY SUNSET + STAR PARTY

### Day Overview
You wake in the highest, greenest sky island in Texas for a sunrise over layered blue-green ridges — and thanks to the Central-time quirk you don't have to get up until 7:15. The day is then deliberately unhurried, because its anchor is at night. Mid-morning you drop 45 minutes down to **Balmorhea**, the 1930s CCC-built spring-fed pool where 15 million gallons a day surge up out of the desert floor — and crucially, this is a **high-sun subject**: only overhead light makes that turquoise depth read. Swim, rinse off the night drive, climb back. The afternoon is frontier Texas — Fort Davis NHS's adobe ruins, the Scenic Loop's ridge panoramas — all setup for the payoff: **McDonald Observatory at 6,300 ft for sunset, then the Star Party** under one of the darkest skies in North America. The waning moon doesn't rise until hours after sunset, so the early-evening sky is genuinely black.

- **Davis Mtns Skyline Drive:** layered blue-green ridges — the greenest mountains in Texas. 70–200mm to compress ridgelines; the CCC-built stone overlook as a foreground element.

## SATURDAY, OCT 31 — SECOND DAVIS MTNS DAWN → GUADALUPE → 🍂 McKITTRICK PEAK COLOR → ⭐⭐ EL CAPITAN SUNSET

### Day Overview
Halloween, and the day the whole date-shift was built for. You take a second Davis Mountains dawn from a different vantage, then make the ~3-hour run north to Guadalupe Mountains NP — **gaining an hour** as you cross into Mountain time. You arrive around midday, which is exactly right: **McKittrick Canyon** is a day-use area that closes at 5 PM, and its golden-red bigtooth maples and cottonwoods against gray limestone walls read best when sun actually reaches the canyon floor. This is the **NPS-designated peak window** (last week of Oct / first week of Nov) and it's the single reason you moved the trip — the only fall color you'll see in the Chihuahuan Desert, and a genuinely unusual combination: **autumn foliage + limestone cliffs + desert scrub in one frame.** Then you save the light for **El Capitan**, the 8,085-ft limestone prow anchoring the range, glowing from the Pine Springs side. Two subjects, two windows, no conflict.

- **★★ El Capitan:** 16–35mm for prow + Chihuahuan foreground (yucca, agave, sotol); 70–200mm to compress the limestone strata; sunstar through foreground vegetation; final minutes = clean silhouette against a burning sky.

## SUNDAY, NOV 1 — ⭐ EL CAPITAN SUNRISE (opt) → CARLSBAD CAVERNS → ⭐⭐ WHITE SANDS SUNSET + BLUE HOUR

### Day Overview
The whiplash day, and the one where **DST ending changes your math**. You gained an hour of sleep overnight, but sunset is now **5:12 PM MST at White Sands, not 6 PM** — so the Carlsbad → White Sands drive has to leave an hour earlier than instinct says. Structure: you're sleeping in Carlsbad, so the Guadalupe sunrise is an *optional* 40-minute backtrack (worth it if El Capitan clouded out Saturday, skippable if you nailed it). Then Carlsbad Caverns gets the middle of the day — correctly, since it's the one major stop that is **not** light-dependent: the Natural Entrance's switchbacking 750-ft descent into the dark, then the Big Room, the largest cave chamber in North America. You're out by early afternoon and driving west, arriving White Sands with time to scout an untracked dune field before the sun drops. Sunset on gypsum, then the cool blue-lavender blue hour that is the most distinctive frame of the entire trip.

### What to Capture
- **★ Natural Entrance:** the switchback trail descending into the cave mouth with the last daylight fading behind. ISO 3200–6400, f/2.8, brace on railings. No flash.

- **★★ White Sands sunset:** low sun rakes the ripples. Camera near the sand, wide, for graphic minimalism. A lone yucca or soaptree as the only vertical. A single footprint trail into untracked white. Then **blue hour** — gypsum goes cool blue-lavender while the sky holds pink; the trip's most distinctive frame.

## MONDAY, NOV 2 — ⭐⭐ WHITE SANDS SUNRISE → EL PASO **DÍA DE LOS MUERTOS** → ⭐ SUNSET → OVERNIGHT HOME

### Day Overview
The best golden hour of the trip, then the cultural finale you didn't plan for. Overnight wind erases every footprint from the dunes, so at 6:25 AM you're shooting **completely untracked gypsum ripples** in the calmest, coolest air of the day, usually with the entire field to yourself — this is the single strongest frame available on this route. Then west to El Paso, and here's the gift: **today is Día de los Muertos**, and you're in a majority Mexican-American border city directly across from Ciudad Juárez. Ofrendas, face paint, processions, and cemetery visits are common in **Segundo Barrio, downtown, and the Mission Trail parishes** — you gave up Austin's Day of the Dead and landed in a considerably more authentic one. You shoot the **Scenic Drive Overlook** (El Paso and Juárez running together as one binational city across the Rio Grande), work the Día de los Muertos street life, eat the trip's best tacos, then close with sunset from **Trans-Mountain Road** — which drops you directly onto I-10 east for the overnight run home.

### ⚠️ MONDAY IS LOCKED — the 6:15 PM departure is a hard deadline

The return math was run against a Tuesday work start. **Junction — not Sonora — is the mandatory sleep stop:** Sonora leaves 210 mi to Austin, which pushes arrival to 8:58 AM. Junction leaves 155 mi → **7:58 AM arrival.** That 55-mile difference is what makes work happen.

| Option | Depart El Paso | Sleep stop | Arrive Austin | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Sunset + DdlM evening processions | 7:15 PM | Sonora, 4h | 8:58 AM | ❌ too late for work |
| **B — Sunset, skip evening processions** | **6:15 PM** | **Junction, 4h** | **7:58 AM** | ✅ **LOCKED** |
| C — Skip sunset entirely, leave 4:00 PM | 4:00 PM | Junction, 6h | 7:43 AM | ✅ fallback, most rest |
| D — Straight through, no sleep stop | 7:15 PM | none | 4:58 AM + 3h at home | ⚠️ 8.7h continuous — unsafe |

**What Option B costs you:** only the *nighttime* Día de los Muertos processions (~7–9 PM). You still get the full daytime DdlM experience — Segundo Barrio ofrendas, Mission Trail altars, calavera face paint, pan de muerto — packed into 11:15 AM–3:40 PM. That's the substance; the evening is the bonus.

**If the evening processions matter more than the sunset:** skip Trans-Mountain sunset, stay in Segundo Barrio until ~7 PM, drive to Junction arriving ~2:20 AM → 3.5h sleep → Austin ~8:40 AM. Only viable if you can start at 9 AM instead of 8.

**Fallback (Option C):** if Monday runs long or you're depleted, depart 4:00 PM, skip the sunset, get 6 hrs in Junction, at desk 7:43 AM rested.

### What to Capture
- **★★ White Sands sunrise:** overnight wind resets the field — completely untracked ripples. Camera low, wide, shooting along a crest into the light. Shadow lines at their sharpest. Your own footprint trail as the only human mark.
- **★ El Paso Scenic Drive Overlook:** the whole binational metroplex below the Franklins, Rio Grande threading between two countries. 24–70mm sweep; 70–200mm to compress the dense border cityscape.

Ask, smile, show them the frame. Your strangers-portrait project applies perfectly here: *"I'm a photographer traveling through — may I make a portrait of you and your altar?"*
