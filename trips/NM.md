## ⏰ THE TIME-ZONE DETAIL (read first — it changes your clock)

- **Austin through Guadalupe Mtns** = **Central (CDT)**. Guadalupe/Pine Springs is Central even though it's far west (Culberson County).
- **Cross into New Mexico** (Carlsbad) = **Mountain (MDT)** — clocks fall back 1 hour. **This buys you time going west** (a noon-Central departure from Guadalupe = 11 AM Mountain arrival at Carlsbad).
- **El Paso is in Texas but on Mountain time.**
- **Driving home Monday**, you cross back to Central near Van Horn — **give the hour back**, so budget the return accordingly.

All stop times below are labeled with their local zone.

## THURSDAY, OCT 22 — AUSTIN → MONAHANS → BALMORHEA → FORT DAVIS → McDONALD OBSERVATORY

### Day Overview
The transition day — Hill Country to sky-island desert in one long westward push. You leave Austin before dawn and let the landscape do the storytelling: prairie gives way to oil country, then the surreal wind-sculpted dunes of Monahans, then the impossibly blue spring-fed oasis of Balmorhea, and finally the climb into the Davis Mountains — the highest, greenest "sky island" in Texas. The whole day is built to deliver you to **McDonald Observatory at 6,300 ft for sunset and the evening Star Party**, one of the darkest, clearest skies in North America. Because you're driving from home, the morning is pure transit with two visual warm-ups (Monahans, Balmorhea) that keep the day from being a freeway grind. Everything here is Central time.

### What to Capture
- **Balmorhea:** the 1930s CCC-built spring pool is impossibly clear turquoise against desert tan — wide for the pool + mountain backdrop; polarizer to cut surface glare and show the depth. A genuine oasis frame you can't get elsewhere on this trip.
- **Davis Mountains (Skyline Drive):** layered blue-green ridges — the greenest mountains in Texas. 70–200mm to compress the ridgelines at late-afternoon light.
- **★ McDonald Observatory:** the white telescope domes catching last warm light on the ridge, then the domes beneath a star-thick sky. The waxing-gibbous moon actually *helps* the Star Party (moonlit domes, bright planets in the telescopes). For a domes-under-stars frame, shoot before moonrise or use the moon as a lit-foreground element. 14–24mm, ISO 1600–3200, 15–20s.

## FRIDAY, OCT 23 — DAVIS MTNS SUNRISE → GUADALUPE MTNS (McKITTRICK + EL CAPITAN SUNSET)

### Day Overview
Your mountain day — but read it as *desert* mountains, not Colorado alpine: dry, exposed, rugged limestone. You open with a Davis Mountains sunrise, then make the ~3-hour run north to Guadalupe Mountains NP, still on Central time. The park's signature autumn draw is **McKittrick Canyon** — golden cottonwoods glowing against dark limestone walls, the one place on this trip that gives you fall color. Critically, **McKittrick is day-use only, closing at 5 PM**, so it's an afternoon shoot, never a sunset location. You save the light for **El Capitan** — the 8,085-ft limestone prow that anchors the whole range — glowing at sunset from the Pine Springs / Guadalupe Peak side. Two different subjects, two different light windows, no conflict. You sleep at Pine Springs, positioned for an El Capitan sunrise.

**Alarm: 6:15 AM (Sat).** Driving: ~230 mi.

**Reality check:** Devil's Hall (limestone-slab canyon) OR McKittrick — doing both plus El Capitan sunset is tight. If McKittrick fall color is peaking, make it the priority and shorten or skip Devil's Hall. McKittrick's parking is limited and fills on fall-color weekends; a weekday (Friday) helps.

### What to Capture
- **McKittrick Canyon:** golden cottonwoods + dark canyon walls + limestone. Don't expect New England — think *golden leaves against desert rock*. Look for stream reflections, backlit leaves, tree trunks against pale limestone. Best when the sun reaches the canyon floor (midday–early afternoon); this is why it's an afternoon, not sunrise, subject.
- **★ El Capitan:** the range's iconic prow. 16–35mm for mountain + Chihuahuan desert foreground; 70–200mm to compress the limestone layers; sunstar through foreground yucca/agave; the final minutes = clean silhouette against a burning sky.

## SATURDAY, OCT 24 — EL CAPITAN SUNRISE → CARLSBAD CAVERNS → CARLSBAD

### Day Overview
A deliberate whiplash day: from the biggest desert mountains straight to hundreds of feet underground. You open with **El Capitan / Guadalupe at sunrise** — the limestone prow catching first warm light from Pine Springs — then make the short 55-mile hop north to Carlsbad Caverns, **crossing into New Mexico and Mountain time (clocks fall back an hour — free time gained)**. Carlsbad is not a golden-hour subject, so you spend the middle of the day underground on the two great self-guided routes: the **Natural Entrance Trail** (a switchbacking 750-ft descent into the dark) and the **Big Room** (a 1.25-mile loop through the largest cave chamber in North America). Back on the surface, the late-afternoon desert around Carlsbad — Walnut Canyon Desert Drive, Rattlesnake Springs — gives you a sunset landscape. You sleep in Carlsbad, positioned for the White Sands push.

### What to Capture
- **★ Natural Entrance:** the switchback trail descending into the mouth of the cave, the last daylight fading behind you. Handheld high-ISO or a compact travel tripod (check current tripod policy). ISO 3200–6400, fast lens f/2.8, brace on railings.
- **★ Big Room:** the great formations — Giant Dome, Bottomless Pit, Hall of Giants — lit by the park's warm path lighting. Long exposures on any stable surface; ISO 1600–3200; white-balance toward the warm cave lighting or correct in RAW. No flash (kills the depth and disturbs others).

## SUNDAY, OCT 25 — CARLSBAD → WHITE SANDS (ALKALI FLAT + SUNSET + MOONLIT DUNES)

### Day Overview
The surrealism day, and the one where you deliberately slow down. The geology flips from underground limestone to a 275-square-mile sea of brilliant white gypsum. You make the ~3-hour morning run west across southern New Mexico to **White Sands National Park**, then spend the day *in* the dunes rather than firing off five roadside frames. Dunes Drive gets you to the heart of the field; the **Alkali Flat Trail** takes you out into the pure, trackless white where the minimalism is the whole point. You hold for the **sunset** — the moment the low sun rakes across the ripples and the dunes glow pink and gold — then blue hour, then, because the moon is up and bright, you stay for **moonlit dunes**: white gypsum under moonlight is its own genre, and it's the right astro play this trip since the gibbous moon rules out deep Milky Way. You sleep near Alamogordo, positioned for a White Sands sunrise.

**Alarm: 5:30 AM (Mon).** Driving: ~170 mi. **Park hours:** White Sands closes at night (no in-park overnight); the park's late-Oct sunset-stroll window runs ~5 PM, aligning with your visit. Shoot until close, then sleep just outside.

### What to Capture — Your Main Event
White Sands ≠ Great Sand Dunes. **Great Sand Dunes = gold + mountains + scale. White Sands = white + minimalism + surreal.** Lean fully into the difference:
- **Ripples & shadow lines** — low sun rakes across the gypsum ripples at sunset/sunrise; camera near the sand, wide, for graphic minimalism.
- **Human silhouette / footprints** — a lone figure on a crest, or a single trail of prints leading into untracked white.
- **Yucca / soaptree** — the only vertical element; use it as a lone subject against the white sea, backlit at sunset.
- **Blue hour** — the gypsum turns cool blue-lavender while the sky holds pink; the most distinctive frame of the trip.
- **Moonlit night** — the gibbous moon makes the dunes glow silver; long exposure, ISO 800–1600, moon behind you lighting the field, a few bright stars above. Settings: f/8, 10–30s.

## MONDAY, OCT 26 — WHITE SANDS SUNRISE → EL PASO → AUSTIN

### Day Overview
The closing day gives El Paso its due, then the long haul home. You open with a **White Sands sunrise** — first light on untracked ripples, the coolest and calmest air of the day, often with no one else in the field. Then you drive ~1.5 hours to El Paso for the trip's final act: a border-city cultural contrast after five days of empty desert. The **Scenic Drive Overlook** on the Franklin Mountains gives you the sweeping panorama of El Paso and Ciudad Juárez running together across the Rio Grande — one continuous binational city at your feet. A quick pass through the historic Mission Trail and downtown, one genuinely great El Paso taco, and then the ~575-mile drive back to Austin (you cross into Central time near Van Horn — give the hour back). This is the accepted long-haul return of a car-sleeper's loop; if you tire, one roadside sleep on I-10 is fine.
