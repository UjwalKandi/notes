### 🌌 What the night sky actually offers in November (honest astro plan)
- **Milky Way galactic core: not a target.** It is only ~7° high at full dark and sets by ~7 PM. Skip core compositions.
- **Milky Way band: yes.** After dark it arcs overhead from Cygnus in the west through Cassiopeia to Perseus in the northeast. By ~10 PM the winter Milky Way (Auriga, Gemini, Orion) is rising in the east.
- **Andromeda Galaxy (M31): near the zenith ~8–10 PM.** Visible to the eye under this sky. A 70–200mm on a tripod catches it.
- **Pleiades:** high in the east by 9 PM.
- **Taurid fireballs:** only ~5 per hour, but unusually bright and slow. Run an intervalometer through any astro session.

The fall-color day, and the last day of the NPS "best" window. A second Davis Mountains dawn, then north to Guadalupe, gaining an hour at the Mountain-time line. **McKittrick Canyon** gets the middle of the day. ## SUNDAY, NOV 8 — ⭐ EL CAPITAN SUNRISE → CARLSBAD CAVERNS → ⭐⭐ WHITE SANDS SUNSET → 🌑 NIGHT IN THE DUNES

### Day Overview
The tightest and most rewarding day. You wake below El Capitan for **sunrise on the prow (6:24 AM)**, then drive to Carlsbad Caverns for the earliest cave entry. The cave gets the middle of the day because it doesn't depend on light: the Natural Entrance's 750-ft switchback descent, then the Big Room. **You must be driving west by ~12:00 PM.** Sunset at White Sands is 5:07 PM and the drive is ~4 hours. Then gypsum at golden hour, a cool blue hour, and, if you hold the backcountry permit, **a night on the dunes under a 4% moon**: the darkest sky of the trip over a white foreground.

**Route to White Sands (~4 h either way):** **(a) Scenic:** Carlsbad → Artesia → US-82 over Cloudcroft through the Sacramento Mountains' pines → Alamogordo. **(b) Highway:** US-62/180 west to El Paso → US-54 north. Pick (a) unless you're behind schedule.

**No permit?** Shoot sunset + blue hour, exit by the posted closing time, and car-sleep in Alamogordo. You lose the dune astro but keep the 10/10 sunset.

**Cuts to protect the sunset:** Walnut Canyon Desert Drive, Rattlesnake Springs, and the King's Palace ranger tour.

### What to Capture
- **★ Natural Entrance:** switchbacks descending into the dark with daylight fading behind. ISO 3200–6400, f/2.8, brace on the railings. No flash. Check the current tripod policy.
- **★ Big Room:** Giant Dome, Bottomless Pit, Hall of Giants under warm path lighting. Long exposures on stable surfaces; correct the warm white balance in RAW.
- **★★ Sunset:** camera near the sand, wide, low sun raking the ripples. A lone soaptree yucca as the only vertical. One footprint trail into untracked white.
- **★ Blue hour:** gypsum turns blue-lavender while the sky holds pink. Probably the most distinctive frame of the trip.
- **🌑 Night:** the white sand reflects starlight, so the foreground reads even without a moon. 14–24mm, f/2.8, ISO 3200, 15–20 s. Compose the dune crest under the Milky Way band to the west, or the Pleiades and winter Milky Way rising in the east after ~9 PM. 70–200mm on Andromeda near the zenith. Intervalometer on for Taurids.
